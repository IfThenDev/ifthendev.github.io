ISBNs

Finding & Understanding ISBNs

Where can I find the ISBN on a book?

The ISBN (International Standard Book Number) can be found in several places:

  • Back cover: Usually above or near the barcode, printed as a 13-digit number
  • Inside front cover: On the copyright page, often listed as "ISBN" or "ISBN-13"
  • Barcode: The barcode itself encodes the ISBN - just scan it!
  • Spine: Some books print the ISBN on the spine near the bottom
Pro Tip

If the barcode won't scan, flip to the copyright page inside the front cover. The ISBN is always printed there in plain text.

Why does my book have multiple ISBNs?

Books often have multiple ISBNs because each format gets its own number:

  • Hardcover - Different ISBN than paperback
  • Paperback - Different ISBN than hardcover
  • Large Print - Has its own ISBN
  • Different editions - Anniversary editions, revised editions, etc.
  • Different publishers - US vs UK editions may differ

If one ISBN doesn't find reading levels, try another! The hardcover edition often has better reading level data than newer paperback reprints.

What's the difference between ISBN-10 and ISBN-13?

ISBN-13 is the current standard (13 digits, starts with 978 or 979). ISBN-10 is the older format (10 digits, used before 2007).

QuickScan accepts both formats. If you have an ISBN-10, you can enter it directly - we'll convert it automatically. Most barcodes use ISBN-13.

The book has no ISBN. What can I do?

Some books don't have ISBNs:

  • Very old books - ISBNs were introduced in 1970
  • Self-published books - ISBNs are optional for self-publishing
  • Library bound editions - May use different identifiers
  • Promotional/advance copies - Often lack final ISBNs

Unfortunately, QuickScan requires an ISBN to look up book data. For books without ISBNs, you may need to check reading levels manually through AR BookFinder or Scholastic Book Wizard websites.

Why wasn't my book found?

If a book isn't found, it's usually one of these reasons:

  • ISBN entered incorrectly - Double-check all digits, especially 0's and O's
  • Very new book - Just-released books may not be in databases yet
  • Out of print - Very old editions may have been removed from databases
  • Regional edition - Some international editions aren't in US databases
  • Network issue - Check your internet connection
Try This

Look inside the book for a different ISBN (hardcover vs paperback). Different editions often have different data availability.

Scanning

Scanning Tips

How do I get the best scan results?

  1. Good lighting - Natural light or bright indoor light works best. Avoid glare on glossy covers.
  2. Hold steady - Keep the book and your device still while scanning.
  3. Fill the frame - Position the barcode so it fills most of the scanner area.
  4. Clean barcode - Wipe off any stickers, tape, or dirt covering the barcode.

The barcode won't scan. What should I do?

If the barcode won't scan:

  • Try manual entry - Tap the keyboard icon and type the ISBN directly
  • Check for damage - Scratched or faded barcodes may not scan
  • Remove stickers - Library stickers or price tags can block the barcode
  • Adjust lighting - Too dark or too much glare can prevent scanning
  • Try the other barcode - Some books have multiple barcodes; one may scan better

Can I switch between front and back cameras?

On iPhone/iPad: Go to Settings in the app and toggle "Use Front Camera" under the Camera section.

On Mac: Go to Settings and select your preferred camera from the dropdown. This is helpful if you have an external webcam.

Reading Levels

Understanding Reading Levels

What reading level systems does QuickScan show?

QuickScan shows up to five reading levels for a book. Some come straight from the publisher. The ones QuickScan works out for itself are marked with ≈.

  • AR (ATOS) — Accelerated Reader book level (for example 4.4). QuickScan looks this up. It is never estimated.
  • Lexile — Lexile measure (for example 680L). QuickScan looks this up. It is never estimated.
  • F&P / GRL — Fountas & Pinnell Guided Reading Level, a letter from A to Z+ (for example R). QuickScan looks it up from Scholastic when Scholastic has it. When Scholastic doesn't, QuickScan works the level out from ATOS or Lexile and marks it ≈.
  • DRA — Developmental Reading Assessment, a number from 1 to 80 used alongside GRL (for example 40). QuickScan reads it from Scholastic's published GRL-to-DRA chart whenever it has a looked-up GRL. If the GRL was estimated, the DRA is too, and both are marked ≈.
  • Grade Level — approximate grade equivalent (for example 4). Looked up when a book data source has one; otherwise worked out by QuickScan and marked ≈.

Alongside those five, QuickScan also shows Interest Level — the publisher's target-audience band (LG, MG, MG+, UG) — and, when available, the AR quiz number, AR points and word count. Those are publisher data about the book rather than reading levels QuickScan works out, which is why they aren't counted among the five.

How can I tell whether QuickScan looked a level up or worked it out?

Every level QuickScan worked out for itself is marked with ≈ — so ≈R is a Guided Reading Level QuickScan worked out, and a plain R is one it looked up. The mark appears on the book detail screen, in your scan history, on the home screen widgets and in the PDF export, and it is only ever applied to a value the app worked out itself. A level a publisher or Scholastic assigned is never marked, however high the letter is.

The mark is text rather than decoration, so it scales with Dynamic Type along with the value beside it. VoiceOver does not depend on the glyph: it speaks the qualifier aloud, reading an estimated level as “Guided Reading Level, estimated, R”.

The CSV export does the same job differently. Instead of the ≈ glyph, it carries a Source column beside each of DRA, GRL and Grade, naming in words where that value came from. See What do the “Source” columns mean? below.

ATOS and Lexile are never marked, because QuickScan never estimates them — they are the values it reads from, not values it works out.

Why does a worked-out Guided Reading Level show as a band rather than one letter?

When QuickScan works a Guided Reading Level out for itself, it shows a three-letter band — ≈G-I rather than ≈H — and the DRA beneath it is read off that same band, so the letter and the number never disagree about the same book.

The band is what QuickScan is willing to claim. A single letter would say the app had picked that letter out of the alphabet on purpose; the band says the book sits in this stretch of the alphabet, which is the honest shape of a converted value. A level QuickScan looked up is never banded — a publisher's letter is shown exactly as the publisher assigned it.

When a GRL is missing

Above P, QuickScan doesn't work a Guided Reading Level out for itself: where Scholastic has no level for a longer chapter book, QuickScan shows none rather than a letter it can't stand behind.

You may still see the other levels for that book — the ATOS level, the Lexile measure and the grade — with an em dash (—) where the Guided Reading Level would be.

Why are some reading levels missing?

Not every book has been assessed by every reading level system:

  • Children's books - Usually have the most complete data
  • Adult books - Often only have Lexile, if anything
  • New releases - May not have been assessed yet
  • Self-published - Rarely have official reading levels

When no source has a Guided Reading Level, DRA or grade for a book, QuickScan works one out from the ATOS level or Lexile measure it does have, and marks it ≈. Above the ceiling described above, it works no Guided Reading Level out at all and shows none.

Where does QuickScan get reading level data?

Two different things happen, and QuickScan tells you which one produced each level.

Looked up. The AR (ATOS) level, the Lexile measure and Scholastic's Guided Reading Level come from book data sources — AR BookFinder, Scholastic and Lexile among them — queried by ISBN when you scan. QuickScan does not keep its own database of books.

Worked out. Where Scholastic has no level, QuickScan uses Renaissance Learning's published ATOS-to-GRL chart. Renaissance describes that chart as giving “reasonable approximations in most cases”, and notes that a book's actual ATOS value “might not correspond exactly to the chart”. Fountas & Pinnell do not publish a chart for converting ATOS or Lexile into a Guided Reading Level, and they consider one invalid: a letter describes more about a book than how hard the words are.

An estimated level is a starting point for choosing a book, not a substitute for a level a publisher assigned. QuickScan never replaces a level it looked up with one it worked out, and every level it worked out is marked ≈.

If a level looks missing

Because levels are queried when you scan rather than stored, a book data source that is temporarily unavailable shows up as a missing level rather than a wrong one. QuickScan's Settings screen has a Book Level Source Health section showing the current status of those sources.

Level My Library

Batch Leveling with Bookshelf Scanner

What is "Level My Library"?

QuickScan can level a whole library that's handed off from our companion app, Bookshelf Scanner. Instead of scanning each book one at a time, QuickScan receives the list of books, looks up each one's reading levels using its usual sources, and sends the levels back — so an entire classroom library can be leveled in a single pass.

How does batch leveling work?

  1. Start "Level my library" in Bookshelf Scanner
  2. Bookshelf opens QuickScan and passes along the books it needs leveled
  3. QuickScan looks up each book's reading levels from AR BookFinder, Scholastic, and Lexile — the same sources it uses for a single scan
  4. The levels are sent back, and you return to Bookshelf to see them on your books

Can I watch progress, and what happens if it's interrupted?

Yes. QuickScan shows progress as it works through the list, so you always know how far along it is. If the process is interrupted — for example, you leave the app — it picks up where it left off. Books that were already leveled aren't looked up again.

Good to Know

Batch leveling uses the same reading level sources as a normal scan, so some books may not have every level available. An active QuickScan subscription is required to look up reading levels.

History

Scan History

How do I view my scan history?

  1. Tap the History tab at the bottom of the screen
  2. Browse your scanned books by date
  3. Use the search bar to find specific titles or authors
  4. Tap any book to see its full reading level details

Does my history sync across devices?

Yes! Your scan history automatically syncs across all your devices (iPhone, iPad, and Mac) via iCloud. Make sure you're signed into the same iCloud account on all devices.

How do I delete a book from my history?

Swipe left on any book in your history list to reveal the delete button, then tap Delete.

How does the Grade Level filter work?

The Grade Level filter in History shows books that match the selected grade using the best available data for each book. It checks three sources in order:

  1. Stored grade - The grade returned directly from reading level databases (e.g., AR BookFinder)
  2. ATOS conversion - If no stored grade, the book's ATOS level is converted to an approximate grade (e.g., ATOS 3.1 = Grade 3)
  3. Lexile conversion - As a last resort, the Lexile measure is converted to an approximate grade (e.g., 500L = Grade 3)

This means books appear in your filtered results even if they don't have an explicit grade, as long as their ATOS or Lexile level maps to the selected grade.

Good to Know

Grade conversions from ATOS and Lexile are approximate. Different reading level systems don't always agree on exact grade equivalents, so use these as a starting point for book selection.

Multi-Scan

Multi-Scan Sessions

How do I scan several books in a row?

Session mode keeps the camera open between books so you can clear a whole stack in one go. Turn it on with the stacked-squares button inside the scanner, then a running session-count badge tracks how many books you've leveled in the current session.

  1. Tap Scan ISBN to open the camera, then tap the stacked-squares button to turn session mode on
  2. Point at a book's ISBN barcode; when the result appears, tap Tap to scan next book to keep going — the session count ticks up
  3. Every scanned book is saved to your History automatically

Is there a limit on how many books I can scan?

QuickScan is free to use with a daily scan limit. When you reach it, an upgrade screen appears — upgrading in the app unlocks unlimited scanning. Your already-scanned books and the current session are preserved when you upgrade.

Good to Know

Upgrades are handled through your Apple Account. You can manage or cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions.

QuickScan for Schools

The Schools edition is fully unlocked — there's no scan limit, no in-app purchases and no subscription. This section applies to the standard QuickScan edition on the App Store.

Gap Report

Classroom Library Gap Report

What is the Gap Report?

The Gap Report shows how your leveled library spreads across reading-level bands, so you can see where you're well-stocked and where you're short on books. It's built entirely from the books already in your History.

How do I export the Gap Report PDF?

  1. Go to the History tab and tap the bar-chart icon at the top to open Library Levels
  2. Choose to export the Gap Report
  3. Share or save the one-page PDF — ready to hand a principal or attach to a DonorsChoose request
Good to Know

The Gap Report shows aggregate band counts only. It never includes student names or individual book titles.

Viewing vs. exporting

You can view your library's level distribution and gaps for free. Exporting the Gap Report PDF is an upgrade feature in the standard QuickScan edition; it's included in QuickScan for Schools.

Export & Import

Exporting & Importing Your Data

How do I export my book list?

  1. Go to the History tab
  2. Tap the share icon (square with arrow)
  3. Choose CSV for spreadsheets or PDF for printing
  4. Share via email, AirDrop, save to Files, or any other method

What's included in the export?

Exports include:

  • Book title and author
  • ISBN
  • All available reading levels (AR, Lexile, GRL, etc.)
  • Date scanned

CSV exports open in Excel, Google Sheets, or any spreadsheet app. PDF exports are formatted for printing with the app name and date.

What columns does the CSV have?

Every CSV QuickScan writes begins with this header row, and the columns appear in this order:

Title,Author,ISBN,ATOS Level,Lexile,DRA,DRA Source,GRL,GRL Source,Grade,Grade Source,Interest Level,AR Points,Word Count,AR Quiz #,Scanned Date

An exported CSV re-imports exactly. An import file you build yourself does not have to match this row column-for-column — see What should the import file look like? below. What each column holds:

  • Title — the book's title.
  • Author — the author, as the book lookup returned it.
  • ISBN — the barcode number. This is the column QuickScan matches on, so it is how a re-import knows a book is already in your library.
  • ATOS Level — the Accelerated Reader book level, as a number (for example 4.4).
  • Lexile — the Lexile measure, as text (for example 680L).
  • DRA — the Developmental Reading Assessment level.
  • DRA Source — where the DRA value in the previous column came from.
  • GRL — the Guided Reading Level letter.
  • GRL Source — where the GRL letter in the previous column came from.
  • Grade — the grade level.
  • Grade Source — where the grade in the previous column came from.
  • Interest Level — the publisher's interest band (for example LG, MG, UG).
  • AR Points — the Accelerated Reader point value, as a number.
  • Word Count — the book's word count, as a whole number.
  • AR Quiz # — the Accelerated Reader quiz number, as a whole number.
  • Scanned Date — the date the book was added to your library.

A blank cell means QuickScan has no value for that column. Title, author, and ISBN are required on import; every other column is optional.

If you have a spreadsheet built against the old export

Before version 6.4 the export had thirteen columns, without the three Source columns. A file in that older shape still imports whole: QuickScan matches columns by name, never by position, so the three columns it doesn't find simply go unasked-for. Nothing shifts and nothing lands in the wrong field.

What do the "Source" columns mean?

Three of the reading levels — DRA, GRL, and Grade — are each followed by a Source column that says where that value came from, using the same wording the app shows on screen:

  • Looked up — the value came from a book data source, not from QuickScan.
  • From Scholastic GRL — the value came from Scholastic's published Guided Reading Level.
  • Estimated from ATOS — QuickScan worked the value out from the book's ATOS level.
  • Estimated from Lexile — QuickScan worked the value out from the book's Lexile measure.
  • Imported — the value came from a CSV you imported, and QuickScan leaves it exactly as you provided it.
  • A blank Source cell means the level beside it is blank too, so there is nothing to attribute.

These columns are there so a level QuickScan estimated is never mistaken for one a publisher assigned. If you are sorting or filtering a class list, the Source column beside each level tells you which values to check against your own records first.

The ≈ mark you see on screen is not repeated in the CSV cells — the Source column carries that information in words instead, so the level itself stays a plain value your spreadsheet can sort and filter.

Can I import a list of books?

Yes. You can import a CSV file to add many books at once — ideal for moving a library between devices, restoring from a backup, or bulk-loading a classroom or school collection without scanning each book.

  1. Open the History tab
  2. Tap the Import button (the download icon in the top toolbar) — or, if your library is empty, tap Import from CSV right on the screen
  3. Pick a .csv file from Files, iCloud Drive, or your device
  4. QuickScan reads the file and shows a summary: Imported · Skipped · Failed

The Import button is always available — you don't need any books in your library first, so it's the quickest way to bring an existing collection into a new install. The importer only accepts comma-separated .csv files; spreadsheets such as .xlsx or .numbers must be saved or exported as CSV first.

After an import

An imported row keeps the levels exactly as you supplied them, and their Source column reads Imported. To fill in the levels and cover art for books that came in without them, tap Find Missing Levels & Covers.

What should the import file look like?

The easiest way to get a correctly formatted file is to export from QuickScan first — an exported CSV re-imports exactly.

If you are building the file yourself, the rule is simpler than it looks: QuickScan matches columns by name, not by position. Your file needs a header row, and it needs Title, Author and ISBN somewhere in it. Everything else is optional, the order doesn't matter, and extra columns QuickScan doesn't recognise are ignored rather than treated as an error. So this is a valid import file:

Title,Author,ISBN
Charlotte's Web,E. B. White,9780064410939
The Giver,Lois Lowry,9780544336261

To bring reading levels in as well, add any of the columns listed under What columns does the CSV have? above, spelled exactly as they appear there. A few details worth knowing:

  • A row missing Title, Author or ISBN is counted as Failed.
  • Leave a cell blank (or write Unknown) for a value you don't have.
  • ATOS Level and AR Points are numbers (for example 4.4); Word Count and AR Quiz # are whole numbers; Lexile, DRA, GRL, Grade and Interest Level are text.
  • Scanned Date is optional; if it's blank or unrecognised, today's date is used.
  • The three Source columns are not read on import. QuickScan does not take your word for where a level came from — anything you import is recorded as Imported.
  • If a title or author contains a comma, wrap that cell in double quotes — for example "Lowry, Lois".

What happens to duplicate books when importing?

Importing never overwrites and never creates duplicates. Any row whose ISBN already exists in your library — or appears more than once in the same file — is counted as Skipped and left unchanged. Only new ISBNs are added. The summary after each import tells you exactly how many books were Imported, Skipped (already present) and Failed (couldn't be read).

Restoring a backup is different

The separate Restore from a backup control on the Import screen updates the books you already have from the file, rather than skipping them — that's how you put reading levels back onto a library you've already rebuilt. It never deletes a book.

Mac

Mac App

How do I scan books on my Mac?

  1. Download QuickScan from the Mac App Store (same purchase as iOS)
  2. Grant camera permission when prompted
  3. Hold a book's barcode in front of your webcam
  4. Reading levels appear automatically
Scanning Tip

Good lighting is especially important for webcam scanning. Position your book in a well-lit area for best results.

Can I use an external webcam?

Yes! If you have multiple cameras, go to Settings > Camera to select which one to use. External webcams often provide better quality than built-in cameras.

What are the keyboard shortcuts?

  • Cmd + S - Start scanning
  • Cmd + I - Enter ISBN manually
  • Cmd + H - View history
  • Cmd + E - Export library
  • Cmd + , - Open Settings

Troubleshooting

Common Issues

The app says "Book Not Found"

This means we couldn't find the book in any of our data sources. Try:

  • Double-check the ISBN for typos
  • Look for a different ISBN in the book (try hardcover vs paperback)
  • Check inside the front cover for additional ISBNs
  • Very new or self-published books may not be in databases

The app says "Connection Problem"

This indicates a network issue. Try:

  • Check your WiFi or cellular connection
  • Try again in a few moments
  • Make sure you're not in Airplane Mode

QuickScan requires an internet connection to look up book data.

My history isn't syncing between devices

  • Make sure you're signed into the same iCloud account on all devices
  • Check that iCloud is enabled for QuickScan in Settings > [Your Name] > iCloud
  • Ensure both devices have an internet connection
  • Try closing and reopening the app

Camera permission was denied

To enable camera access:

  1. Open your device's Settings app
  2. Scroll down and tap QuickScan
  3. Toggle Camera to ON
